Modelling blood cancer

  • Leukemia is a cancer of the blood. For one type, a daily pill keeps it under control for years
  • every 3 months a blood test gives one number: how much cancer is left
  • plotted over the years, every patient becomes a curve
  • my job: reproduce the patient's course using a model, for hundreds of patients
Institute for Medical Informatics and Biometry, TU Dresden / Uniklinikum Dresden. Schematic, simulated patient.

What if we give half the dose?

  • once the equations fit, we can play through things nobody has tried on real patients
  • for example: cut the dose in half after three years
  • the prediction: for most patients the cancer stays under control
  • fewer side effects, and the pill costs more than a car per year
  • a clinical trial later tested exactly this
Fassoni, Baldow, Roeder, Glauche 2018, Haematologica; Karg, Baldow et al. 2022, Front Oncol. Schematic curve.

Data Scientist @ Dataqube

  • online shop. A/B test: half of the visitors see the old page, half a changed one. Then we count who buys
  • 0.3 percentage points more buyers sounds like nothing. With 400k visitors a month it is a six-figure sum per year, in either direction
Learning: small changes move big margins. Measure them.
Illustrative numbers, simulated data. The students will run this kind of test themselves in the A/B testing lab.

Who are our users, actually?

  • every dot is a customer: how often do they order, how much do they spend
  • the computer finds the groups, we give them names. The "average customer" is none of them
  • each group got its own shop flow and offers
  • and each group reacted differently in the next A/B test
Learning: one number for everyone hides the story. Segment first, then decide.
Simulated data.
