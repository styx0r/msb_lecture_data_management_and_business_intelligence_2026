So what is AI?

"The study is to proceed on the basis of the conjecture that every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it."

Dartmouth summer research proposal, 1956. The paper in which the term "artificial intelligence" was coined. jmc.stanford.edu

Artificial Intelligence is the simulation of intelligence, especially learning.

Nothing about robots, nothing about chips, nothing about ChatGPT. A machine that behaves as if it were intelligent. Whether it "really" is intelligent is a philosophy seminar, not this one.

Three ways to learn

Supervised

with an answer key
  • data: inputs x plus the right answer y (the label)
  • goal: predict y for new x
  • output a number: regression (delivery time, demand)
  • output a category: classification (fraud yes/no, churn)

Unsupervised

no answer key
  • data: inputs x only, nobody labelled anything
  • goal: find structure
  • groups: clustering (customer segments)
  • the odd ones out: anomaly detection

Reinforcement

trial, error, reward
  • data: none at the start, the agent creates it by acting
  • goal: maximise reward over time
  • examples: game AI (AlphaGo), robots, dynamic pricing
  • and: how ChatGPT learned manners (afternoon)
Ask one question of any AI project: is there a label? Yes: supervised. No: unsupervised. Nobody knows the right answer until we act: reinforcement.

What the machine just did: training

1. Predict

\( \hat{y} = w \cdot x + b \)
  • the model is a line with two parameters
  • start with random w and b

2. Measure the error: loss

\( L = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2 \)
  • mean squared error (MSE): the average squared distance between line and points
  • one number, lower is better

3. Update: gradient descent

\( w \leftarrow w - \eta \, \frac{\partial L}{\partial w} \)
  • the gradient says in which direction the loss grows
  • move opposite to it, by a step of size η: the learning rate

4. Repeat

until L stops improving
  • each round: predict, loss, update
  • too large a step: it explodes, too small: it takes forever
This recipe, predict → loss → update → repeat, is all of machine learning. A neural network has millions of w's instead of one. ChatGPT has a trillion. Same loop.
  • 2 min.
  • Exam: MSE is the loss for regression.
  • Exam: gradient descent moves opposite the gradient; learning rate = step size.
  • Exam: training stops when the loss stops improving.
  • Takeaway = bridge to leaderboard and optional neural networks deck: a stacked line.
  • Networks not in the exam; the four facts are.
  • Abbr.: MSE = mean squared error.

k-means in four moves

1. Initialise

  • you choose k
  • drop k centroids at random

2. Assign

  • every point joins its nearest centroid
  • that is the only "decision"

3. Update

  • move each centroid to the mean of its points
  • hence the name

4. Repeat

  • until the centroids stop moving
  • usually under 20 rounds
Output: a cluster number for every customer, nothing else. The algorithm does not know what "premium" means. It also does not know k. Both come from you, and both go into a BI dashboard afterwards.
  • 1 min.
  • Exam: the four moves (initialise, assign, update, repeat).
  • Limitations: k is a human choice, names are a human choice.
  • Result depends on the random start: run twice, sometimes differs.
  • Bridge to BI: segments = a warehouse column and a dashboard filter.
  • Abbr.: k-means = k cluster means (centroids); BI = business intelligence.

6. Reinforcement: learning by doing

No answer key, no fixed dataset. An agent acts, the environment answers with a new state and a reward. The agent updates its policy: what to do in which state.

ShopNow: which discount to show this visitor? Reward: margin of the order. After a million visitors the policy knows more than any marketing rule.

Famous: AlphaGo (2016) learned Go by playing itself. Boston Dynamics robots learn to walk in simulation first.

This afternoon: ChatGPT's last training step is reinforcement from human feedback: people rate answers, the rating is the reward.

Costly to run on real customers: every bad action costs money. Hence simulators, and small traffic first, like Saturday's test phase.

  • 2 min, one slide.
  • Exam: reinforcement learning learns by rewards and feedback.
  • ShopNow link to A/B test: A/B = one-shot "which variant earns more".
  • Reinforcement agent asks continuously, shifts traffic as it learns (bandits).

Takeaways

  • AI is the simulation of intelligence, especially learning. Symbolic AI: humans write the rules. Machine learning: the rules are learned from examples. Deep learning: with neural networks. Generative AI: the output is new content. Generative is not general.
  • Three acts: rules (1956–1990s, two winters), learning from data (1990s–2012), deep and generative (2012–). The ideas are old, data and compute made them work.
  • The money: private AI investment ×60 since 2013, 345 bn in 2025, half of it generative. Big Tech spends more on data centres in 2026 than the moon landing cost. Adoption faster than the PC or the internet.
  • Three ways to learn: supervised (labels: regression gives a number, classification a label), unsupervised (no labels: k-means gives a cluster number), reinforcement (rewards). First question of every project: is there a label?
  • Training = predict, loss (MSE), update opposite the gradient by the learning rate, repeat until the loss stops improving. Same loop from a line to ChatGPT.
  • Before you believe a model: where is the data from, what is the label, how was it tested.
